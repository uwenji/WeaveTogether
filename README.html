WeaveTogether — weave a lattice cell by cell

A practice for the interactive weaving website: TryTriWeave's lattice in, a step-by-step weaving plan out. Static files, no build, made for GitHub Pages. 31 October 2026; second version 1 November after the Design update (whole laths, friction clips, material in cm, joint overlap, the two-layer History bar); third version 2 November after Design update 2 (sub-steps, Paul Tol's light scheme with a vivid band, the path on the canvas, one Options sidebar, the stroke and joint bugs); fourth version 4 November after Design update 3 (the fan: every crossing of the cell woven, free ends included; the notation — one strip at a time with its direction and its over / under sequence; the two-row History bar in place of the step card; the camera aligned with the closing strip; the finished weave in full colour); fifth version 5 November after Design update 4 (signage arrows after Calvert & Kinneir; the strip on top shown whole with a dot at the crossing; a strip marched on from cell to cell; cells woven by their neighbours left out of the tour; the direction toward the crossing made next; the order along the strips and the start from the rim; the woven side on top in the aligned camera; the srf, torus and tree cases); sixth version 7 November after Design update 5 (the laths on a smooth spline, as WeaveSim renders them; the highlight on the strip itself — path line, passed bar, current arrow — and the strip as a tape in the History bar; the scale and the bounding box in File; the layer chips; the number of sides in the chips; the pan and the disc-click bugs); seventh version the same evening after Design update 6 (a cell is one event: a crossing between strips in hand is made inside the strip's sub-step, right after the strip has passed both of its strips; no empty first tick; the first strip hangs; the crossing sections coloured as soon as the crossing is made; the arrow before the crossing, its stem the bar itself; the tape gone; the start layer gone).

TryTriWeave decides where the cells of a triaxial weave go on a shape and how many sides each one has. This practice takes that lattice (the OBJ it exports) and answers the weaver's questions: where to start, which cell to close next, which strips meet there and which one goes on top, which crossings to clip until they hold, where to cut a strand that is too long, and how to read the singular cells as a map before starting. Building a cell weaves its strips completely through each other: every crossing of the cell's fan (its ring and the opposite edge of each of its triangles) that is not made yet — so the free ends are woven, not just laid across each other (your "it intersects each lath, but it is not woven"). A cell is built in sub-steps, one strip at a time, as in your notation: first the strip that arrives from the cell before is continued through the fan (the marching strip), then the strips already in hand are crossed where they meet at this cell (lay — the strip on top is the subject), then every new strip is added in turn — threaded through the strips in hand in its weaving direction, its crossings listed as an over / under sequence — and the last sub-step closes the cell; a cell already woven by its neighbours is counted but left out of the tour; a final sub-step shows the finished weave in full colour. The History bar plays the sub-steps and, inside one, the crossings. Every strip in hand is shown whole: its woven part on the surface, the rest hanging straight off its outermost crossing.

Run it

cd singular_path
python3 serve.py                   # http on port 8095; prints this machine's URL and the Wi-Fi URL for other devices
python3 serve.py --open            # … and opens the page; --port 9000 for another port, --verbose to log every request
open http://localhost:8095/          # #cylinder, #Bunny, #cone5 … open on a case

Any static server works too (python3 -m http.server); the page fetches models/*.obj, so file:// does not. serve.py sends Cache-Control: no-store, so an edited module or a re-exported lattice is always refetched.

GitHub Pages: push this folder as it is (for example as the repository's root or docs/). Everything is relative: index.html, style.css, js/, vendor/ (three.js r170 + OrbitControls, the same files as WeaveSim), models/ (the eight lattices), README.html. Any triangulated OBJ whose vertices are the cells can be opened or dropped on the view; TryTriWeave's "Export lattice" is the intended source.

What is computed

1 · lattice → weave js/weave.js

The lattice is the dual triangulation: a vertex per cell, a triangle per small triangular opening. Every lattice edge is a crossing (two laths meet at its midpoint), every triangle holds three short lath segments (its mid-segments), and the n segments around a vertex close an n-sided cell. A lath passes through edge midpoints and in every triangle takes the mid-segment that wraps the vertex it did not wrap in the previous triangle. The tracing, the over/under parity (over and under alternate along a strand, the two strands at a crossing differ, fixed by a parity BFS) and the three-family colouring are the ones of TryTriWeave/web/js/lattice.js (traceStrands), re-implemented here and left untouched there. On top of it: which strand and which position every segment belongs to, and the ring of segments and crossings around every cell in cyclic order. Face windings are made consistent first (one triangle of the exported Bunny was flipped) and turned outward.

2 · the plan js/planner.js

rulewhat it doeswhy / to discuss
startmost reachable (default): the cell from which half of the singular cells are nearest — the k-th nearest singular cell is nearest, k = ⌈S/2⌉, ties broken by the sum of distances, then by preferring a regular cell, then the higher cell. Also centre (smallest sum of distances to every singular cell), from flat (regular cell farthest from any singularity), from the rim (the lowest cell of the boundary — a tube starts at its bottom ring; a closed surface has no rim and falls back to most reachable, the status says so), or pick a cell on the surface (Shift-click, or the Pick start button).A cone starts at its singularity; the capped cylinder starts flat at a cap's centre and reaches the cap's six 5s together, then the body — your item 1 and 2. The Bunny's default start is a 7 in its densest region; the pick is there for your item 4. The rim is your Design update 4: the construction direction of a tube is from its foot.
orderspiral: rings = graph distance from the start; inside a ring the next cell touches the cell just closed and has the most crossings already made; a ring is finished before the next begins. along the strips: follow one strip cell by cell along its crossings — the row it runs through — one way, then the other way from where it was picked up; when it is exhausted take the next strip introduced so far (closed strands first, then the longest), and when none of those has a crossing left, the nearest cell of the front and the newest strip it introduces. fewest new strips: the same front as the spiral, but a cell whose sides are strips already in hand comes first. singular to singular: the patch grows toward the singular cells one after another along a nearest-neighbour tour, then finishes in rings. The readout counts strip pick-ups, moves to a cell not touching the last one, and the most clips held at once, so the strategies can be compared.The spiral closes cells early (Beady's "no loose bead") and keeps the hands where they are: no jump on the cylinder, 10 on the Bunny. Along the strips is your Design update 4 (the spiral "count[s] about marching the momentum and topology direction" wrong): on the 230-cell tube from the rim it weaves the first ring, the second, the diagonals between them, the third … The price is clips: a diagonal laid across a ring is only held once the next ring crosses it, so the tube holds up to 69 clips at once (spiral: 8); the 216-cell tube, whose strands are columns, marches up a column at a time and holds 8. "Fewest new strips" barely changes the pick-ups (every piece is picked up once anyway) and jumps a lot — a sign that the cost of introducing a strip needs your definition (see the questions). "Singular to singular" reaches the singular cells early at the price of a long front.
fanThe crossings a cell makes are those of its fan — its ring edges and the opposite edge of each of its triangles — that no earlier cell has made. The strands of the fan are exactly the cell's ring strands: a hexagon weaves its six strips through each other at all twelve crossings (each strip crosses the four strips of the other two families twice), a pentagon its five at all ten. The free ends beyond the ring are therefore woven as the cell is built, and the next cell finds part of its fan done.Design update 3, "Weave": with the ring alone the strips only intersected beyond the cell. Your notation drawing counts the crossings this way (strip 6 of the first cell makes four).
sub-stepsPer cell, in order: continue — the strip that arrives from the cell before (the most recently added strip in hand whose crossings here follow on from the ones it has made) is threaded on through the fan, beyond its woven end; then add — each new strip in turn, threaded through the strips in hand (those from earlier cells and the strips added just before at this cell), its crossings in the strip's weaving direction as an over / under sequence. A crossing of the fan between strips already in hand (read from the latest strip: "S7 under S3") is made inside the cell's strip sub-step — the marching strip's, else the first new strip's — slotted in right after the strip has passed both strips of that crossing (the later of its crossings with them; after the nearer one when it passes neither), so a cell is one event and the under / over reads in the order of the hands (your Design update 6: cone 4, "over, under, over, S7 under S3, under"); such crossings get a lay sub-step of their own only when the cell has no strip to thread. The last sub-step closes the cell (its chip carries the cell's polygon). A cell whose fan was already woven by its neighbours is left out of the tour (the readout counts them: "30 woven by their neighbours"; its disc on the surface still jumps to the sub-step that completed it); after the last cell a done sub-step shows the finished weave. Every crossing is a tick inside its sub-step (a fractional sub-step), so the sequence can be played crossing by crossing; a sub-step starts at its first crossing (no empty "nothing yet" tick), and the first strip of the weave, which has no crossing to make, hangs straight through the place of the crossing it will make first. Clips as before: a crossing only held by friction is clipped when made; the clips come off when both strips hold.Your notation (notation_system.png), read left to right: one strip per panel, the over / under tags below, the chips. Design update 4: "once strip add, should weave the cell in same row" — the continue sub-step is the strip marching along its row; your Q1 / Q2 answer dropped the empty closings. Open: whether continue and lay should be one sub-step (see the questions).
directionA strip is threaded toward the crossing it makes next: of its two ends, the one on the side where the nearest crossing still to make belongs to the earlier cell of the order (the direction the weaving will take it); a continued strip goes on beyond its woven end; when neither side has a crossing left, the end with less length to pull through. The crossings are ordered from its first crossing on (→) or from its last one back (←) accordingly; the signage arrows on the strip show it and ⮌ in the History bar reverses it (kept per strip and cell while the lattice is open).Your Q3: "align cell order … should be better align the direction". The length rule from Design update 2 is the fallback.
clipsA strip holds itself by friction once it has been woven through N crossings (4 by default, 5 for slippery material; Map → material → holds after). A crossing is a clip point until both strips through it hold.Your Design-update rule. It cut the clips held at once from 38 to 12 on the cylinder and from 86 to 17 on the Bunny (spiral order, stock 12 a, overlap 2). The earlier rule (a crossing locked once both its cells are closed) was a graph proxy in the sense of Kollsker's LEGO connectivity; this one is the material's.
stripsLengths in centimetres: the lattice edge a in cm sets the scale, the length limit is the longest strip the material gives. A strand longer than that is cut into pieces; consecutive pieces overlap by k crossings (the joint, 0–4; the second piece runs doubled on top of the first through them). from the start: full-length pieces from the first-woven crossing, the remainder last; even: equal pieces. A cut is moved off a segment beside a singular cell. S7.2 = strand 7, piece 2; crossings are numbered along the strand from where the weaving first meets it.Your item 4 / Design-update material block. The X (a strip crossing itself) is gone: the over / under sequence says it ("over … (itself)").Your "cross exists" note: only in the cell < 6 local region; Design update 3 removed the X.

3 · the view js/view.js

TryTriWeave's idiom (status bar · view with the navigation cube · one Options sidebar with Display, 1 File and 2 Discretization — resizable by its edge, docks left or right, folds to a tab; the step card is gone, its weave info lives in the History bar) with laths as swept rectangular sections with real thickness along a centripetal Catmull-Rom spline through the crossings of the strand (six sections per segment, ten in a joint; consecutive segments share their section at the crossing, so there is no gap or notch where the lath bends — your render_update-07, WeaveSim's render); under laths lie on the surface, over laths climb onto them, the height blends between crossings. Colours (Paul Tol's qualitative schemes, the "light" set as you preferred over "pale"): every piece has one hue of the light scheme for its woven part and the vivid band of the same hue — darker, more saturated, as in his banded scheme. States, as your notation (render_update-09, after Calvert & Kinneir's motorway signage): the strip being added or continued is vivid along its whole length with a thin black centre line on its top face (path), the part threaded in this sub-step from its first crossing to the current one as a thick bar (passed), and a bold arrow at the crossing of the current tick pointing in its weaving direction (current); the marks sit on the strip and follow it over and under — where the strip runs under another, so do its line, its bar and its arrow (your "the highlight follows the strip, not above the strip … the arrow will go under"); only the dots of a lay sit on top of their crossing. At a lay a dot marks every crossing of the sub-step (made: small black; current: bigger; to come: pale), no strip is highlighted whole, and the strip threaded just before at this cell keeps its path and its bar, so the crossing that closes the cell reads with the strip that brought it. The arrow stands before the crossing, its tip at the edge of the crossing strip, so the crossing itself shows who is over by colour; its stem is the bar itself, which follows the strip from the first crossing of the sub-step (a short way back at the first tick) up to the head — one continuous mark (your canvas change). Everything woven at the current cell is vivid (the segments whose crossings were made here, the colour running one section past each crossing so it does not stop at the lattice edge); the crossing section — a fifth of the segment on either side of a crossing — is laid, drawn and coloured as soon as that crossing is made, whatever the state of the segment's other crossing ("more sections for crossing"), and a hanging remainder leaves the strip past it, the woven parts of earlier cells are light, the part of a strip in hand that is not woven yet is grey — hanging straight off its outermost woven crossing along the material's tangent and as long as what is left (default), or along its future path on the surface, or hidden (a strip just picked up, with no crossing yet, lies along its path); at the final sub-step every strip is vivid, in "by family" too (your bug 2). The active cell's badge is its number on a dark disc while the cell is being built and hollow once it is closed; the singular cells are discs with their number of sides. A clicked strip gets a dashed stroke and everything else is dimmed (Esc clears); the strip readout is gone, as you asked. Layers (Display): surface, lattice, singular, path (the path of the order with the hollow dots of the regular cells closed — one chip now), active, laths, highlight (the marks above), clips, cuts, bounding box (the start sphere is gone; the start cell is the golden chip). The scale lives in 1 File: × model units → centimetres (× 1 reads the file's units as centimetres), or type one of the three sizes of the bounding box and the object is scaled to it; the lattice edge a in centimetres follows, with the strip lengths and the pieces; the box with its sizes is the bounding-box layer. The strokes are drawn above every lath; in a joint the piece picked up later runs on top of the one already there, narrower, and comes down to the surface past its end. Every vertex carries its fractional sub-steps (laid, inside the woven extent, picked up, gone, the two crossings and the cells at which they were made) and its piece id, so scrubbing by sub-step or by crossing is a handful of uniforms; only the strokes and arrows of the sub-step are rebuilt. Marks: a ring on every crossing while it is a clip point, the joint lines, the path and the cell dots, the singular discs. A click on the bare surface does nothing (Shift-click picks a start); a cell disc tells about its cell in the status bar without moving the history (your bug 2); a lath selects its piece.

Camera (Display → camera): fixed; follows: pan — the view slides to the cell, direction and distance stay, and the world is upright again (the pan used to keep the up axis of an earlier aligned view, which read as a rotation — your bug 1); follows: align with the strip — once per cell the view turns so that the strip that closes the cell (its side of the cell, in its weaving direction) runs along the screen's X axis and the cell's normal is the screen's up, and the woven side of the cell is at the top of the screen, the new strip at the bottom (your "eye + hands → new strip → woven object"): the crossings of the fan already made lie on the woven side, the ones still to make on the other, and that decides which way the view tilts; when they lie along the strip (a strip marched along its row) all the cells built so far decide. The tilt off the normal is 34° when the aligned view starts and your own afterwards (orbit to change it, 11°–69°; the distance is always yours); between cells the move is one smooth motion (smootherstep easing, 0.45–0.8 s by distance, no sudden acceleration), and you can still orbit, zoom and pan around the cell. Home resets the up axis to Z.

Questions to settle before the website

Order strategies you listed. Spiral, most reachable / flat / picked starts and singular-to-singular are in. Three I need defined before coding them: cover surface more first — close the cells that extend the patch the farthest (a BFS front, the opposite of close-early) or the ones that touch the most unwoven cells? strip less intersection — prefer steps whose new crossings are made by strips still short in hand (fewer bends), or avoid crossings between strips already in hand (no threading under existing strips)? continue curvature first — finish a singular cell's ring before moving on (so the dome / saddle forms while it is reachable), or follow the curvature ridge of the surface?
The cost of introducing a strip. "Fewest new strips" as a greedy barely changes the number of pick-ups (every piece is picked up once anyway) and makes the hands jump. What should the planner minimise instead: the number of strips in hand at once (open ends to manage), the number of times a piece is threaded under existing strips, the hanging length at each step, or the pick-ups themselves grouped (several strips at once)?
Continue and lay. A cell now reads: continue the marching strip (its own arrows), then cross the other strips in hand (one lay sub-step with a dot per crossing), then add. On the 230-cell tube that is 177 continued, 174 lays, 55 added. Should the continue be folded into the lay (one sub-step per cell, the marching strip first in it), or stay a sub-step of its own with the arrows, as now?
Cells woven by their neighbours. They are out of the tour now (tube: 30 of 230, srf: 113 of 365, torus: 302 of 696 along the strips). Their discs still jump to the sub-step that completed them. If you want a checkpoint for them after all (a count, a ✓ chip), it is one option (skipEmpty).
Ring by ring costs clips. Along the strips from the rim, the 230-cell tube weaves ring, ring, diagonals, ring — your picture — but a diagonal laid across a ring is held by nothing until the next ring crosses it: up to 69 clips at once against 8 for the spiral. Is that the real cost of this order (a clip per diagonal, which is how a basket is started), or should the order take the diagonals two rings at a time?
The 216-cell tube. Its strands are columns and diagonals, no rings, so "along the strips" marches up a column (27 of them) rather than around. Is that the intended construction direction for that topology, or should the front stay horizontal there (the spiral does)?
Direction rule. "Toward the crossing made next" follows the order; a continued strip always goes on beyond its woven end. Where neither side has a crossing left the length rule decides. If a fixed convention is better for the tape (always the head end), it is one line.
Aligned camera. Up = the cell's normal, X = the closing strip, the woven side on top. On a tube woven from the rim the view therefore looks from above with the rim far away — the tube upside down on screen — which is "hands near, object far" but not gravity. If you would rather keep the world upright, X can align with the strip's projection only.
⋈ scope. Now the closing of every cell under six carries it (the crossing tags too, in that cell's card). If you mean only the one crossing where the two parallel strips actually meet, say which: the family colouring from TryTriWeave puts its conflicts anywhere on the surface, not at the 5s, so it cannot be used as it is.
Cutting and annealing (item 4). Cut positions follow the lattice (crossing counts and chord lengths). WeaveSim's annealer would move them on the relaxed geometry; porting it is straightforward (the strands here are polylines through crossings, as its StrandGeometry expects). Decide on the lattice (now) or on the relaxed geometry (later)? And is a joint allowed inside a singular cell's ring?
Physics. Kept out, as you asked. What is in: the hanging remainder along the tangent, and per piece the number of crossings it is bent through, the in-plane turning and the surface turning along it. If a breaking material needs more, the next step would be a per-piece bending-energy estimate from the lath section and the crossing geometry — still no solver.
Sub-assemblies. The Bunny's single spiral still holds up to 17 clips with the friction rule; ears, head and body as separate seeds joined at seams would shorten the front. Segment by hand (click regions) or automatically?
Material defaults. a = 5 cm and an 80 cm limit are placeholders. What are your laths (width, thickness, length, material), and how do marks go on (sticker, print, laser)?
